FAMILY & FRIENDS

Stand beside someone you love.

Much of sickle cell can't be seen from the outside. Knowing what to say, and what to do on a bad day, can change that day for them.

WHEN IT IS A BAD DAY

Three things that help most

01

Make it safe to be open

Create an atmosphere where they can be open about how they feel and what they need. Listen without judging. Sickle cell often shows nothing on the outside, so let them tell you.

02

Go with them

Be a second pair of ears. Ask to see their care plan and keep a copy. Take them to the emergency room and speak up if they can't. A crisis can come on fast and be inconvenient. Never let them feel like an inconvenience.

03

Stay after

Crisis leaves people drained. Rest, quiet company and no questions can be the best help.

WHAT TO SAY

Small words matter

Helpful to say

  • “What do you need right now?”
  • “You can talk to me about it, any time.”
  • “Do you want me to come with you?”
  • “You are such a resilient person.”

Better not to say

  • “But you look fine.”
  • “Can't you just push through?”
  • “Are you sure it's that bad?”

DON'T BE AFRAID TO ASK — the more people ask what helps, the more they'll know.
