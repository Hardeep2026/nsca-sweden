PARENTS & CARERS

You don't have to navigate this alone.

A diagnosis changes a family's days. Here are the first steps, how to talk to school, and where to find other families who understand.

AFTER A DIAGNOSIS

Three first steps, at your pace

01

Understand it and know your care team

  • Sickle cell disease is a genetic blood disorder. It is not contagious, and nobody caused it.
  • Ask your child's doctor to explain it in plain words. Write your questions down before you go.
  • Ask for a care plan for your child, made with their doctor. Keep a paper copy, and take it to the emergency room.
  • Ask who your main contact is, and how to reach them out of hours.
  • Go to every check-up, even when your child feels well.
  • Know the warning signs, and when to call the clinic or go to the emergency room. A fever is urgent.
02

Tell the people around your child

  • You decide who to tell, and how much. Not everyone will understand, and that is OK.
  • A short way to say it: “Our child has sickle cell disease. It is a genetic blood disorder. Sometimes they need rest, water or hospital.”
  • Tell your child's school and daycare, and give them a copy of the care plan.
  • The Discrimination Act (Diskrimineringslagen) protects your child at school. If the school does not give the help your child needs, that can be discrimination.
03

Day to day, for both of you

  • Make sure your child takes their medicine. Be patient and understanding when they can't.
  • Don't take your own emotions out on your child.
  • Your stress can make a crisis harder. Your child needs you calm.
  • Ask for support from other parents, a friend, or us.
