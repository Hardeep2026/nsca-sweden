ADULTS LIVING WITH SICKLE CELL

Know your care. Know your rights.

Simple answers about your care, the emergency room, work and everyday life in Sweden, so you spend less time explaining and more time living.

START WITH WHAT YOU NEED

Things adults ask us

Going to the emergency room

  • Keep your care plan with you, on paper or on your phone.
  • Say “I have sickle cell disease” as soon as you arrive, and show your plan.
  • Say how much pain you are in (0 to 10).
  • Ask them to call your haematologist. Bring someone who can speak up for you.

Work, sick leave and benefits

  • You decide who at work to tell, and how much.
  • The Discrimination Act (Diskrimineringslagen) protects you at work. If someone treats you worse because of your condition or won't give you the help you need, that can be discrimination.
  • Ask your employer for what helps: rest breaks, flexible working, time off for appointments.
  • Ask your doctor for a medical certificate for when you are off sick.
    [CONFIRM: sick leave and Försäkringskassan rules]

Speaking up for your care

  • Your pain is real. You do not have to prove it.
  • Your care plan helps staff take you seriously because it comes from your own doctor.
  • If you do not feel listened to, ask to speak to a senior doctor.
  • Ask a partner, family member or friend to come with you and speak up when you can't.

WHY THIS PAGE EXISTS

Good healthcare makes living possible.

“I have lived with sickle cell disease for 45 years. And I know from experience what makes the difference. When your care is consistent, when the people around you understand your condition and you trust them, something profound shifts. You stop bracing. You start living. You build a career, raise a family, pursue the life you want, because the foundation is there. That is not a luxury. That is what good healthcare makes possible. And it is what every person with sickle cell in Sweden deserves.”

Toni Forsberg Founder & Chair, NSCA Sweden
